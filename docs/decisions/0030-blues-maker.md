# 0030: The blues maker, with sounds from ElevenLabs

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-11-22. Adds `/jam/` to the page list (PLAN.md section 3). Builds on the 12-bar strum (ADR 0018).

## Context

Joe asked for an interactive blues song maker: pick the instruments, the beat and the rest, with the sounds generated by ElevenLabs.

The site has no server (ADR 0027), the ElevenLabs key is a secret (CLAUDE.md rule 9), and generating audio for every visitor would spend credits on a public page that anyone could hammer. Generating a whole song per visitor would also give no control over tempo or key, so nothing could be changed while it plays.

## Decisions

- **Generate the sounds once, not per visitor.** `npm run jam:sounds` (`scripts/make-jam-sounds.ts`) asks ElevenLabs' sound-effects API (`/v1/sound-generation`, `eleven_text_to_sound_v2`) for 28 one-shots listed in `src/data/jam.yaml`: two drum kits, two basses, three guitar tones, piano, organ, harmonica, tenor sax, and two room loops. It runs on Joe's machine with the `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` already in his shell, shared with his other projects. The key never reaches the site, a build, or GitHub. The originals are committed in `src/assets/jam-source/` (about 1.3 MB, never served), so tuning them again costs no credits, and the build needs no key.
- **The browser is the band.** `src/lib/jam.ts` (pure, unit tested) turns a song (key, feel, tempo, form, a tone or nothing per part) into what each part plays in each bar: kick on 1 and 3, backbeat on 2 and 4, a walking or boogie bass that steps a half step into each chord change, the Jimmy Reed root-fifth-sixth comp on guitar, rootless voicings on keys, and fills from the blues scale that answer in bars 3–4, 7–8 and 11–12 (the call and response ADR 0018 was waiting on). `src/scripts/jam.ts` schedules it a bar ahead with Web Audio. Because the timing and the notes are ours, it stays on the beat and in key, and any change takes effect at the next bar.
- **Tune the notes ourselves; don't trust the prompt.** ElevenLabs mostly ignores the note a prompt asks for. It leans toward C, in whatever octave it likes, even with the frequency written into the prompt, and the harmonica came back on C5 every time. So the script measures each original's real pitch (YIN, `src/lib/audio-analysis.ts`), then renders a tuned sample every three semitones across everything the part can play, by TD-PSOLA pitch-shifting the nearest clean original (`src/lib/psola.ts`). PSOLA keeps the note's length and body, where speeding a sample up would also shorten it and thin it out. Every tuned sample is measured again and must land within 15 cents. Shifts of up to 16 semitones pass. The browser then picks the nearest tuned sample and bends it at most a semitone and a half, which a unit test checks for every note in every key and feel. That's 78 tuned samples from 18 originals, plus the drums and room loops: about 2.3 MB in all in `src/assets/jam/`. A song downloads only the samples its key and feel use.
- **A song is a link.** The controls are a plain GET form, mirrored into the address bar, so a song can be shared.
- **Honest about what it is.** The page says the sounds were generated with ElevenLabs and aren't Johnny or his band, and it points to the shows. It makes no claims about Johnny, so it needs nothing from the facts ledger.

## Why it passes the "worth it" test

It doesn't build itself from Johnny's data, so it has to serve an audience the home page can't. It does: people who play with the strum and want more, and anyone who wants to hear how a 12-bar fits together, without bloating the home page. The home page keeps its budget. Its only change is a "Play it with a band" link under the strum. The page's script and sounds load only on `/jam/`, and only the sounds the song uses (a few hundred KB for a full band).

## Consequences

- One more page in the sitemap. It isn't in `llms.txt`, which is about Johnny.
- No new third parties at runtime and no CSP change: the sounds are same-origin, hashed under `/_astro/`.
- New typeface, color or motion: none. The playing bar is a red fill, a state change, not an animation.
- Regenerating a sound costs ElevenLabs credits and changes the measured pitch, so rerun the unit tests after `npm run jam:sounds`.
